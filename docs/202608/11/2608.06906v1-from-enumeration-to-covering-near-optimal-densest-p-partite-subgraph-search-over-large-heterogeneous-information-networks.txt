## ABSTRACT 

Given a heterogeneous information network (HIN) and a query meta-path P of length 𝑖 , the densest P-partite subgraph problem finds the subgraph, spanning the 𝑖 typed layers of P, that maximizes a parameter-free density: the number of meta-path instances over the geometric mean of the layer sizes. It has applications across bibliographic, e-commerce, and biomedical networks. The state-of-the-art approximation linearizes the geometric-mean objective by fixing per-layer weights, but solves one subproblem for every feasi-ble weight set, of which there are O ( ( 𝑛 /𝑖 )𝑖 ), and on each achieves only a 1/𝑖 approximation. We show that neither the exhaustive enu-meration nor the loose guarantee is necessary. First, we replace enumeration by covering: polylogarithmically many representative weight sets, localized further by a data-dependent bound, cover all feasible ones while losing only a tunable factor 1+𝜂 in density. Second, we cast each fixed-weight subproblem as a weighted su-permodular densest-subgraph instance and solve it near-optimally, lifting the overall guarantee to (1−𝛿 )/( 1+𝜂 ). To our knowledge, this is the first near-optimal density approximation beyond the bipartite (𝑖 =2) case, and it yields a PTAS for every fixed 𝑖 . Algorithmically, our solver is an adaptive peeling scheme that never materializes the meta-path instances, whose number can exceed the graph size by orders of magnitude. Incumbent-driven pruning further discards representative weight sets before their subproblems are solved. Experiments on five real HINs show that our algorithms achieve substantial speedups over enumeration-based baselines, and can further certify the near-optimality of the returned subgraph.

Cohesive P-partite subgraphs. A P-family is a tuple of vertex sets, one per type in P; the subgraph it induces, the P-partite graph, is typically large and sparse, while its meaningful information is hidden in the dense regions. Mining cohesion across multiple vertex sets has driven a long line of models, from multipartite cliques [12] and cross-graph quasi-cliques [24] to multilayer cores and dens-est subgraphs [4, 10], the relational community [17] and influen-tial community [31]. Among these models, the densest P-partite subgraph is especially appealing: a simple, parameter-free query surfacing cohesive structure across every type in P, with no size, degree, or pattern threshold to tune. ## 4 SOLUTION OVERVIEW 

Our approach builds on a simple cover-and-conquer principle that decouples the two difficulties of DPSS : searching the iRM configura-tion space and solving each fixed-weight subproblem. Rather than enumerating every iRM-set, we partition the iRM polytope Π𝑛 into a small number of non-overlapping cells, solve a single representative iRM-set in each, and return the densest family found. Algorithm 1 is parameterized by two components, each supplied by a later section: a partition of Π𝑛 into disjoint cells 𝐶 1⊔···⊔ 𝐶 𝐾 (§5), and a cell solver Solve (𝐺, P,m) returning a P-family for a fixed iRM-set m (§6). It never searches for m∗; it solves one representative per cell, on the premise that the cell containing m∗ already yields a near-optimal family. Three questions decide whether the premise holds.

Recovered density. Figures 4(a) and 4(b) report the density actually recovered, 𝜌 /𝜌 ∗, as 𝜂 and 𝛿 vary. The first observation is that every variant recovers the optimum: on the core datasets 𝜌 /𝜌 ∗ ≥0.999 at the default 𝜂 =0.1 (A7 within 1.5×10 −3), and on the breadth datasets the near-optimal solver returns the exact optimum at every setting. A faint ordering A3 ≳ A5 ≳ A7 also appears across 𝛿 : the un-reduced first-order net represents more weight-sets, so its best cell lands slightly closer to the optimum, but the gap ( ≤0.2% ) is within the (1+𝜂 ) tolerance. 

Achieving vs. certifying near-optimality (A6/A7 vs. A8). 

Whether a subproblem is peeled to the fixed 1/𝑖 cap (A6), to the heuristic near-optimal stop (A7), or to the worst-case (1−𝛿 ) dual-gap certificate (A8), all three recover the same density (Figure 4(a)). The reason is that on real inputs the peel reaches a near-optimal subgraph within a few passes, so certifying it only proves the result rather than changing it. Consequently, certification yields a guarantee rather than accuracy: A6 and A7 achieve near-optimal density but cannot certify it on a given input, whereas A7’s end-to-end (1−𝛿 )/( 1+𝜂 ) bound, secured by A8’s certificate, holds whatever the data (§6.3 shows an input where the uncertified 1/𝑖 

peel falls short). Therefore, A6/A7 is preferable when the input is trusted and A8 when a worst-case guarantee is required.

## 9 RELATED WORK 

Densest subgraph and its variants. The densest subgraph, which maximizes |𝐸 (𝑆 ) |/| 𝑆 |, is exact via parametric max-flow [11] and 1/2-approximable by greedy peeling [2]; its variants span directed/bipartite [19], density-friendly [7], 𝑘 -clique [15, 28], multi-layer [10], anchored [6], and locally densest clique/triangle [29, 30] density, recently consolidated in a unified analysis [32] and a broad survey [20]. DPSS is their 𝑖 -partite meta-path generalization. 

Near-optimal supermodular solvers. A fast line recasts the ob-jective as a supermodular ratio solved to (1−𝜀 ): Greedy++ [1], iterative peeling [3], scalable decomposition [13] and its lexicographically-optimal-base Frank–Wolfe view [14], coordinate descent [23], and, for the directed case, convex programming [22], scalable discovery [33], and accelerated coordinate descent [21]. All are one-dimensional, tied to the single ratio at 𝑖 =2. We lift them to 𝑖 >2 via our weight-polytope cover (§5, §6). At 𝑖 =2 they scan the ratio at a first-order pitch ( O ( log 𝑛 /𝜀 ) guesses, each within 1+𝜀 [22]); our √𝜂 net (§5.2) reaches the same 1+𝜂 loss in O ( log 𝑛 /√𝜂 ). For 𝑖 >2 the feasible weights lose their total order (§3), so the ratio search no longer applies. 

Dense subgraphs over HINs. HINs model typed, multi-relational data queried through meta-paths [25–27]; cohesive-structure dis-covery there centers on query-anchored community search [8, 9, 17, 31], with recent methods even avoiding meta-path materializa-tion [18], closely related to our instance-free peeling. > 𝜂
